## 1.1 BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY
Documents have long been used to record information, establish ownership, communicate decisions, and preserve records for future reference. Early documents existed in physical forms such as clay tablets, papyrus, parchment, and paper, which were stored in archives, libraries, and record rooms and shared through manual copying, physical transfer, or controlled institutional access[1][2][3]. While effective at the time, these methods were constrained by limited physical space, susceptibility to damage, slow retrieval, and restricted accessibility, highlighting the need for more efficient ways to manage information. This need led to the process of digitalization, transforming physical records into digital documents, enabling information to be created, stored, and accessed electronically. Digital documents are typically stored in local systems, organizational servers, and cloud-based platforms and are shared through digital networks, such as email and web-based applications [28][29].

Despite improved efficiency and accessibility, digital document storage introduces challenges such as centralized storage risks, unauthorized access, data breaches, and data integrity concerns. Reports indicate that the global average cost of a data breach has reached $4.4 million, while many organizations still lack proper access controls, leaving them vulnerable to ransomware, social engineering, and third-party attacks [5] [6]. The Thales Data Threat Report 2025 further emphasizes that data breaches remain prevalent in increasingly complex digital environments [4]. These challenges underscore the growing need for secure, controlled, and reliable digital document management, which has driven the adoption of cloud-based storage systems offering scalability, accessibility, and support for decision-making [7] [8] [9].

While cloud-based platforms offer significant benefits, persistent security challenges remain, particularly in terms of system reliability, data confidentiality, and user authentication [10]. Despite the efficiency of widely used platforms such as Google Drive, recent phishing-related breaches and security warnings highlight ongoing risks [11]. Incidents involving major providers demonstrate that even platforms with advanced protections can be compromised. For example, a Google Drive breach caused by social engineering affected nearly 40% of files containing sensitive information, exposing gaps in access controls and data privacy management [12] [13]. Similarly, iCloud experienced large-scale credential exposures through malware-infected devices [30] [31], Dropbox suffered a breach of its Dropbox Sign service, exposing user account data [32] [33], and OneDrive, in one of its security reports, documented design flaws that could allow unauthorized access [34] [35]. Collectively, these cases highlight persistent vulnerabilities in cloud-based document storage and the ongoing need for robust security measures.

In response to the risks inherent in cloud environments, organizations have adopted various security measures, among which encryption has emerged as a prominent mechanism for protecting digital documents. Hybrid models integrating encryption with access controls are widely implemented to ensure that even if unauthorized access occurs, the confidentiality of stored files remains preserved [14][19]. Other complementary approaches, such as blockchain-based auditing [15][16] and machine learning–driven monitoring [17][18], further strengthen layered defenses. Nevertheless, encryption remains the foundational safeguard in most cloud storage systems [39], operating under the assumption that intercepted or breached files remain unreadable without the corresponding decryption keys. While this assumption enhances confidentiality, it does not entirely eliminate risk.

Despite its strengths, encryption alone does not fully prevent unauthorized recovery of sensitive information. Cybersecurity research has acknowledged that encrypted data, once obtained by adversaries, may still be subjected to unauthorized decryption attempts through reverse engineering, brute-force strategies, or exploitation of implementation weaknesses [40][41]. In cloud environments, encrypted files may be exfiltrated through credential compromise, insider threats, or system vulnerabilities [42][43], after which decryption efforts can occur outside the protected infrastructure. In such situations, encryption continues to preserve ciphertext form, yet the security boundary shifts from the protected system to the encrypted artifact itself. When ciphertext becomes independently accessible, adversarial efforts may focus on exposing the original content without interacting further with the originating environment [44][45]. This reveals a structural limitation in conventional encryption-based models, where confidentiality depends primarily on cryptographic strength, even when encrypted files are no longer contained within the secured platform.

Beyond encryption, cryptography–steganography (crypto-stego) integration has emerged as a promising approach for secure document storage by simultaneously encrypting sensitive content and concealing its existence [20][21]. By hiding encrypted data within benign cover files, crypto-stego systems add an additional layer of obscurity beyond traditional cryptographic protection. However, these systems face practical challenges. Steganographic techniques perform best with small payloads; however, the embedding of substantial data volumes or high-entropy encrypted payloads can strain the system's capacity and reduce overall efficiency [22][23][24]. Moreover, the increased technical complexity can affect usability, as users may experience difficulties performing secure upload, retrieval, or file management tasks, as observed in recent web-based and application-level platforms [25][26][27]. Hybrid systems also complicate cryptographic key handling. Poor key management has been documented in real-world cases, such as the Codefinger ransomware attack on AWS S3 buckets, API key exposure in Dropbox Sign, and unrotated compromised keys in Microsoft SharePoint, which allowed unauthorized access to encrypted files and sensitive information [36][37][38]. Although crypto-stego systems improve confidentiality and concealment, issues such as embedding large payloads, maintaining user-friendly operation, and ensuring secure key handling continue to pose obstacles, highlighting the need for innovative security models that address these operational complexities.

To address the limitations identified in current cloud-based document security approaches, this study proposes a cloud-based web application built on a reconstruction-dependent security architecture for digital document storage. This architecture ensures that decryption is structurally dependent on successful reconstruction of distributed encrypted data fragments, while incorporating the integration of cryptography and steganography and implementing lightweight key management to enhance security and mitigate practical challenges related to payload capacity, usability, and key handling.

## 1.2 OBJECTIVES OF THE STUDY
The primary objective of this study is to develop StegoLock, a cloud-based web application for secure digital document storage that implements a reconstruction-dependent security model integrating steganographic encryption and enhanced key management mechanisms. The system aims to reduce reliance on traditional key-dependent security by ensuring that decryption is not solely dependent on key possession but also on the successful reconstruction of distributed encrypted data segments. Through this approach, the application seeks to address vulnerabilities associated with key exposure and unauthorized reconstruction of encrypted data. Additionally, the system will provide an efficient, secure, and user-friendly platform for sharing digital documents, while protecting both the confidentiality and concealment of sensitive information, optimizing steganographic payload capacity, maintaining compatibility with widely used document formats, and ensuring reliable performance in resource-constrained environments.

To be specific, this capstone project aims to:
1. Implement AES-based encryption with a KDF-based key management process to ensure the confidentiality and integrity of a document file.
2. Design and implement a segmentation process that splits the encrypted document into multiple segments and hides them through a steganographic embedding process into cover files, which are scattered across the application’s cloud storage to enhance security.
3. Develop a web-based application that implements and integrates the AES-based encryption, segmentation, access control and authentication, and sharing mechanisms to a document storage platform.
4. Evaluate the application based on ISO/IEC 25010 quality characteristics to assess the effectiveness in terms of functional suitability, security, reliability, and measure usability and performance efficiency.

## 1.3 SIGNIFICANCE OF THE STUDY
The main objective of this study is to develop StegoLock, a cloud-hosted web application for secure digital document storage and sharing that employs a reconstruction-dependent security model. The system combines AES encryption with KDF-based key management, segmentation of a document’s encrypted data into multiple fragments, and concealment of these fragments using multi-technique steganographic embedding across multiple cover files, which are then scattered within the cloud storage environment. Unlike conventional document storage systems, StegoLock requires all encrypted data fragments to be successfully extracted from the cover files and reconstructed before decryption is possible, ensuring that unauthorized access to partial data provides no meaningful information. The primary beneficiaries of the project are the following:

*   **Organizations Handling Sensitive Digital Documents** - Businesses, educational institutions, healthcare providers, legal offices, and government agencies that manage confidential records will benefit from StegoLock’s reconstruction-dependent security model. The system not only encrypts sensitive documents but also conceals and disperses their segmented data across multiple cover files, reducing the risk of data breaches, interception, and unauthorized access in cloud storage environments. This segmentation also allows the steganographic embedding to handle larger document payloads without compromising security or performance.
*   **End Users and Professionals** - Individual users and professionals who frequently store or share confidential documents online will benefit from a secure yet user-friendly design. By enforcing access control and requiring full reconstruction of all encrypted data fragments, StegoLock protects the confidentiality and integrity of sensitive information during storage and sharing, ensuring that partial access provides no meaningful data.
*   **Future Researchers** - This study provides a reference for researchers in information security and cryptography-steganography integration. The reconstruction-dependent model, together with segmentation and lightweight key management, offers insights into designing hybrid security systems that balance robust protection, usability, and practical key management. Additionally, segmenting the encrypted data improves the payload capacity for steganographic embedding, allowing large documents to be securely concealed across multiple cover files without exceeding embedding limits.

## 1.4 SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS
StegoLock is implemented as a cloud-based web application accessible through modern web browsers, allowing users to create accounts, log in, manage, and share documents within a secure environment.

Upon upload, documents are compressed, encrypted using AES in Galois Counter Mode (AES-GCM) with KDF-based key management, and segmented into multiple fragments. These fragments are then concealed via steganographic LSB embedding across diverse cover files. Decryption is permitted only after successful authorization, followed by the complete extraction and reconstruction of all fragments. This segmentation strategy enables the system to handle original files up to 5MB by distributing the encrypted payload across multiple cover media. For clarity, this entire security workflow is defined as 'locking,' while the retrieval process is termed 'unlocking.' Additionally, the system provides a hierarchical folder structure to ensure an organized and user-friendly document management experience.

Furthermore, StegoLock supports .pdf, .doc/.docx, and .txt document formats. The steganographic cover pool is system-provided and administrator-managed, encompassing image (.png), text (.txt), and audio (.wav) formats. Upon registration, each user is allotted an initial storage quota of 225MB, which is calculated based on the cumulative 'in-cloud' size of the resulting stego-files rather than the original document size. Due to the overhead inherent in steganographic embedding, the total storage footprint will significantly exceed that of the source documents. To maintain system stability and ensure efficient resource allocation, the application is optimized for limited concurrent processing; consequently, larger files or multiple simultaneous operations may result in extended processing durations. Furthermore, access is strictly limited to authenticated users, with a secure access-control mechanism ensuring that shared documents are restricted to designated collaborators. Finally, while the system is built for modern web standards, usage on legacy or unsupported browsers may result in reduced performance or visual inconsistencies.

## 1.5 DEFINITION OF TERMS
This section provides operational definitions of key terms used in this study, particularly those directly related to the design, implementation, and evaluation of StegoLock. These definitions clarify how specific concepts are applied within the system, ensuring a shared understanding of the terminology throughout the study.

*   **Digital Document Storage** - a component of a document management system responsible for holding and organizing digital files, ensuring that documents are maintained and accessible to authorized users.
*   **Reconstruction-Dependent Security Architecture** – refers to a security framework where decryption is structurally impossible unless all distributed encrypted data fragments are successfully retrieved and reassembled.
*   **Cryptography-Steganography System** – also known as a crypto-stego system, is a hybrid security approach that combines the principles of cryptography and steganography, emphasizing a protection mechanism that encrypts sensitive content and conceals its existence within ordinary-looking cover files.
*   **AES-GCM (Advanced Encryption Standard in Galois/Counter Mode)** – an authenticated encryption method employed by StegoLock to secure documents, providing confidentiality, data integrity, and protection against tampering.
*   **Key Derivation Function (KDF)** – a cryptographic algorithm used in StegoLock to generate strong encryption keys from user-provided secrets, supporting lightweight and secure key management.
*   **Lightweight Key Management** – the system within StegoLock that handles the creation, distribution, and storage of encryption keys efficiently, minimizing complexity while maintaining strong security for users.
*   **Steganography** – the method used to conceal encrypted document data within cover files, ensuring that the existence of sensitive information is hidden from unauthorized parties.
*   **Steganographic Embedding** – the operational process of integrating encrypted document fragments into cover files (images, text, or audio), such that the hidden data remains protected from detection.
*   **Cover File** – the file used to hold embedded encrypted document fragments; it appears ordinary to external observers but contains a hidden payload accessible only by authorized users.
*   **Segmentation** – the systematic process in StegoLock that divides an encrypted document into smaller pieces or fragments prior to steganographic embedding, ensuring that sensitive information is distributed across multiple cover files.
*   **Fragments** – the individual pieces of encrypted data generated during the segmentation process; each fragment is stored within a separate cover file and is essential for the reconstruction of the original document.
*   **Stego File** – the final digital artifact stored in the cloud upon completion of the locking process. It serves as the structural anchor for the unlocking workflow, requiring both its retrieval and valid user authentication credentials to initiate the extraction of the hidden encrypted fragments stored within.
*   **Locking** – the integrated multi-stage security pipeline in StegoLock encompassing document compression, AES-GCM encryption, segmentation, steganographic embedding, and secure cloud storage.
*   **Unlocking** – the retrieval and reconstruction process that fetches stego files from the cloud, extracts hidden fragments, reassembles the encrypted payload, and performs authorized decryption and decompression to restore the document to its original state.
